\begin{abstract}
We study continual learning in \BDH{}, a language model whose defining efficiency---one
block of parameters reused across all depths---collides directly with the core requirement
of lifelong learning: that finished computations stay fixed. Three findings organize the
problem. \emph{First}, sequential training forgets catastrophically (later phases cost
earlier languages $+1.6$ to $+2.2$ nats), the failure persists under literally frozen old
parameters, and it therefore cannot be explained by parameter drift: \textbf{weight
isolation does not imply computation isolation}. \emph{Second}, the condition that makes
continual serving exact is structural: the grown map must leave the old-task reachable set
invariant and agree with the old map on it. No soft or hard gate can substitute for this
structure in a generic frozen path; but \BDH{}'s additive growth rule \emph{constructs} it,
so an input-dependent selector---hard or soft---selects an isolation that already exists.
\emph{Third}, the resulting system separates acquisition, serving, and consolidation rather
than seeking one universal mechanism. Measured on 100M-parameter models across five languages
and three text registers: prefix selection reproduces specialists to within evaluation
precision, with a 128-token likelihood detector at 100\% accuracy; a training-free softened
mixture serves mixed streams within 0.003 nats of the discrete oracle; merge$\,\to\,$random-
scatter-prune$\,\to\,$brief replay consolidates everything into one fixed-width model equal
to joint co-training ($\pm0.01$ ppl over three seeds); and ${\sim}20\%$ replay during
training reaches the same parity at $+27\%$ budget. Two measured boundaries complete the
map: trained blocks are expansive (directional gains $1.05$--$1.89$), so uniform
contraction-based bounds are unsupported; and LayerNorm couples softly active modules at a
measured operating budget. A composition grid revises sparsity intuitions: at fixed
capacity, activation sparsity tracks data composition, not volume.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

How should a trained network remain trainable? The continual-learning literature offers
three families of answers: protect important parameters (EWC~\cite{ewc}, SI, MAS), store and replay experience~\cite{agem}
experience, or allocate separate capacity per task (progressive networks~\cite{pgn}, PackNet~\cite{packnet}). Each
presupposes that preserving \emph{parameters} preserves \emph{computation}. In ordinary
feed-forward networks the distinction is academic; in depth-recurrent architectures---which
reuse one block across all depths, obtaining depth without parameter count---it is the whole
story.

This paper takes \BDH{}-GPU as its instrument. \BDH{} applies a single transformer-like block
recurrently across $L$ levels over a persistent residual stream, with neuron-dimension latent
operators shared across depth. Its uniform scaling in the neuron dimension makes it uniquely
amenable to \emph{additive growth}: new neurons append, nothing else changes. We show that
this property, properly formalized, is what makes continual learning tractable here.

We ask one question in three readings:
\begin{quote}\itshape
Can input-path interference arising from shared sequential composition be eliminated,
bounded, or made controllable by an input-dependent gate or projection, without per-depth
trainable parameters and without retraining the frozen path?
\end{quote}

\noindent\textbf{Answer (\S\ref{sec:theory}--\S\ref{sec:recipe}).} Exact elimination is
possible precisely when the grown map keeps the old-task reachable set invariant and equals
the old map on it. A generic frozen path has no such structure, and no gate creates it.
\BDH{}'s growth rule constructs it; detection and selection exploit it. Where the structure
is absent, we find no evidence supporting useful uniform bounds---trained blocks are
expansive on the tested trajectories---so the honest alternatives are structural separation
or replay. The resulting system separates three concerns usually conflated:
\textbf{acquisition} (grow into fresh capacity), \textbf{serving} (route to, or softly mix,
the stored phases), and \textbf{consolidation} (merge, prune, brief replay). Each concern
has a measured solution; none requires a miracle.

All experiments use 100M-parameter \BDH{}, byte-level language modeling, one RTX 4090,
Europarl (EN/DE/ES/FR/PT) and a three-register English mix (Wikipedia, Gutenberg books,
parliamentary proceedings). Core comparisons are within-corpus and seed-replicated
(\S\ref{sec:recipe}).

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Architecture primer}\label{sec:primer}

Table~\ref{tab:notation} fixes notation. One \BDH{} level computes, with encoders
$E,E_v\in\mathbb{R}^{n_h\times d\times N}$ and decoder $D_c\in\mathbb{R}^{(n_hN)\times d}$,
\[
u=\operatorname{relu}(x@E),\qquad
v=\operatorname{relu}\bigl(\operatorname{LN}(a(u))@E_v\bigr),\qquad
x \;\leftarrow\; \operatorname{LN}\Bigl(x+\operatorname{LN}\bigl((u\odot v)@D_c\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
where $a(\cdot)$ is per-neuron attention over positions of $x$: each latent neuron attends
independently, there are no query/key/value projection matrices, and every operator above is
coordinate-separable across neurons. The only cross-neuron coupling is LayerNorm's global
statistics. One triple $(E,E_v,D_c)$ serves all $L$ levels.

\textbf{Growth} appends zero-initialized neurons to the triple; existing neurons, the token
embedding, and the output head are frozen. RoPE frequencies carry the neuron count in their
exponent, so naive growth would silently rewrite every surviving neuron's phases; our growth
path preserves the frequency prefix verbatim.

\subsection{Continual protocol}
Phases are ${\sim}30$\,MB byte streams; each phase initializes from the previous endpoint
(weights only; fresh optimizer). Evaluation is per-domain held-out perplexity under a fixed
cold random-crop protocol, always within-corpus: cross-corpus loss comparisons are void
(Europarl is intrinsically easier than WikiText-class text).

\subsection{Definitions: state- and output-level isolation}
For old-task inputs, define the state-level interference at depth $\ell$,
$\Delta^{(\ell)}_A(x)=h^{F'}_\ell(x)-h^{F_A}_\ell(x)$, and the output-level interference
$\Delta^{y}_A(x)=R(h^{F'}_L(x))-R(h^{F_A}_L(x))$ under the shared readout $R$. We take
\emph{state-level} isolation ($\Delta^{(\ell)}=0$ for all $\ell$) as the primary criterion:
output-level equality can hide internal interference that resurfaces under distribution
shift. Exact isolation means state- and output-level equality for all $x\in\Xa$ and all
depths; $\varepsilon$-isolation means the supremum is bounded by $\varepsilon$ uniformly in
depth and phase count. Weight isolation ($\dot\theta_A=0$) is a third, weaker property;
\S\ref{sec:phen} shows it implies neither of the other two.

\section{The phenomenon: four negative results}
\label{sec:phen}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{figures/forgetting.pdf}
\caption{Sequential training EN$\to$DE$\to$ES at 100M. Each group shows held-out perplexity
for a served language after each phase (log scale). Earlier languages degrade by
$4$--$9\times$ while each new language reaches specialist quality.}
\label{fig:forgetting}
\end{figure}

\textbf{N1 --- Sequential training forgets catastrophically.} Two later phases cost EN
$+1.59$ nats and DE $+2.15$ nats above their phase peaks, identically under ReLU and hard
top-$k$ activations ($k\ge10\%$); see Figure~\ref{fig:forgetting}. The strongest direct
statement is: forgetting persists when the old parameters are frozen, so it cannot be
explained by drift in the frozen subset. We interpret the residual-stream contamination this
induces as \emph{computational interference}; ``representational overwrite'' is our shorthand
for the measured end-state.

\textbf{N2 --- It is not optimizer shock.} Constant-LR schedules without warmup/decay
restarts are strictly worse: peaks degrade and retention \emph{decreases} by
$+0.09$--$0.21$ nats. Annealed, settled endpoints consolidate better.

\textbf{N3 --- Protection cannot fix it.} Per-neuron importance gating (mean $|xy|$, union-max
across phases, $\alpha\in\{0.5,0.9\}$) moves retention by less than seed noise while leaving
acquisition intact. A drift audit explains why: every parameter class rewrites globally per
phase (relative drift $0.6$--$1.3$), and importance is spread thin (99.9\% of neurons above
1\% of maximum). There is no localized important subset to freeze.

\textbf{N4 --- Freezing cannot fix it either.} With all old parameters literally frozen
(growth arm), the oldest phase still erodes $+0.84$ nats per subsequently added block
(EN: $2.26 \to 5.40 \to 12.49$ ppl after one/two grown blocks). New modules contribute
additively into the residual stream that deeper levels feed back through frozen encoders:
\[
\underbrace{\Delta\theta_A=0}_{\text{weight isolation}}\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
\underbrace{F'\big|_{\Xa}=F_A\big|_{\Xa}}_{\text{computation isolation}} .
\]

Two positive anchors emerge from the same runs. First, new languages \emph{acquire} at near
parity using only fresh capacity (+25\% width reaches within 0.09 nats of specialist
quality). Second---decisive for everything below---\textbf{hard suffix masking reproduces
each phase's specialist numerically identically under the evaluation protocol}. The
architecture contains a candidate solution; \S\ref{sec:theory} states the condition under
which it is exact, \S\ref{sec:recipe} measures it.

\section{Theory: when does selection give exact isolation?}
\label{sec:theory}

Statements are proved in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. We label each result
\textsc{proved}, \textsc{measured}, or \textsc{conjectured}, and keep those labels strict:
measurements never discharge a missing assumption.

\begin{theorem}[dissociation]\label{thm:dissoc}
\textsc{proved.} There exist extended maps $F'=F_A+c\,\mathbb{1}$ (any $c\neq0$) whose
parameter set contains $\theta_A$ unchanged, yet whose old-task trajectories differ from the
specialist's at every depth. Parameter isolation imposes no bound on computation isolation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[exact-isolation criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
\textsc{proved.} Let $\TA=\bigcup_{\ell\le L}\{h^{F'}_\ell(x):\ x\in\Xa\}$ be the reachable
old-trajectory set. Then
$F'^L(x)=F_A^L(x)$ for all $x\in\Xa$, $0\le\ell\le L$
\;\textbf{iff}\;
$F'(z)=F_A(z)$ for all $z\in\TA$.
A sufficient structural decomposition: with $H_A=\operatorname{im}P_A$ containing $\TA$,
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(C1)] \emph{invariance}: $F'(H_A)\subseteq H_A$, equivalently $(I-P_A)F'P_A=0$;
\item[(C2)] \emph{restriction equivalence}: $P_AF'P_A = P_AF_AP_A$ on $H_A$.
\end{enumerate}
Neither condition alone suffices: under (C1) alone the confined dynamics may differ from the
specialist (e.g.\ $F'=2\,\mathrm{id}$ commutes with any $P_A$ yet rescales trajectories);
under (C2) alone later leakage re-perturbs confined states.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[prefix growth constructs the structure]\label{cor:prefix}
\textsc{proved} (from separability facts S1--S2, Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). Under hard
suffix masking, the grown map restricted to masked forwards satisfies (C1)--(C2)
identically: suffix write-backs vanish, so $F'$ reduces to $F_A$ on the whole space. Hard
selection therefore reproduces specialists exactly. Under \emph{soft} activity, LayerNorm's
global statistics break condition (C1) at first order in the suffix magnitude --- the
commutation residual $C_P$ becomes nonzero --- producing the measured leakage budget of
\S\ref{sec:recipe}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[zero-forcing]\label{lem:zf}
\textsc{proved.} If suffix blocks are individually gated, $f_b\mapsto g_b(x)\odot f_b$, then
exact preservation on $\mathcal{X}_A$ forces $g_b(x)=0$ wherever $f_b$ is nonzero along the
preserved trajectory. (The specialist term is un-gated; only suffix activity is constrained.)
Gate freedom lives on never-active coordinates and new-task inputs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[soft gates cannot be exact]\label{prop:soft}
\textsc{proved} (counterexample class). For non-affine updates with cross-coupling, the
preservation constraints form a functional equation over the trajectory with no solution in
an open gate range: a two-dimensional ReLU system makes the requirement
$(1{+}g_1)^2+g_1g_2\varepsilon\delta=4$ for all input magnitudes $a>0$, unsolvable by any
input-independent pair. Exactness collapses to the hard endpoint; soft regimes can only
bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[depth amplification]\label{prop:amp}
\textsc{proved} (standard recursion). With local injections $\delta_\ell$ and Lipschitz
level update constant $L_f$, $\|e_L\|\le\sum_j(1+L_f)^{L-1-j}\delta_j$. Uniform-in-depth
bounding additionally requires contraction ($\rho<1$) on the interference dynamics.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[expansiveness: measured, not proved-impossible]\label{rem:exp}
\textsc{measured.} Directional spectral-norm proxies of trained \BDH{} levels at old-input
states are $1.05$--$1.89$ (median per level). This is evidence \emph{against} the
contractivity premise of Proposition~\ref{prop:amp} for the tested models---uniform
contraction-based bounds are unsupported---and \emph{not} an impossibility theorem: isolated
stable directions may coexist with expansive medians.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[selector vs.\ creator, scoped]\label{cor:sel}
Within the frozen-path projection mechanism studied here, gates do not create invariant
computational structure; they select structure already present in the frozen operator. In
broader architectures a learned gate can alter effective computation; that possibility is
outside this mechanism class.
\end{corollary}

\section{The recipe, branch by branch}
\label{sec:recipe}

\subsection{Grow to acquire}
Growth adds ${\sim}2$k neurons/head (~25\% width) per phase; new languages reach
$2.22$--$2.33$ ppl---within 0.09 nats of specialists---without touching any old parameter.
Acquisition is not the bottleneck; erosion is, and Remark~\ref{rem:exp} records that the
tested models give no support for bounding it away in place.

\subsection{Select to serve}
\label{sec:serve}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Hard selection}: mask suffix blocks per detected phase. Numerically
indistinguishable from each specialist under the evaluation protocol; active width
$\le\times192$ (three-language stack), $\le\times224$ (three-register stack).
\item \textbf{Detection}: pooled-embedding logistic regression (compiled detector) and
128-token likelihood scoring both reach \textbf{100\% accuracy}, across languages and across
registers of one language. Context-sufficiency holds empirically.
\item \textbf{Soft serving} (\emph{empirical $\varepsilon$-isolation; separate from the
theorem above}): logit mixture $p=\sum_r w_r p_r$,
$w_r=\mathrm{softmax}(-\mathrm{NLL}_r/\tau)$. \emph{Protocol}: 16 held-out crops per domain;
routing scores are mean cross-entropy over the first 128 positions of each prefix-masked
forward; the served metric is cross-entropy over the remaining positions of the mixed
distribution; $\tau\in\{0.05,0.1,0.25,0.5,1,2\}$. At $\tau\le0.25$ the mixture is within
\textbf{0.003 nats} of the discrete oracle; at $\tau=0.5$ it still beats unrouted serving by
${\sim}1.7$ nats; no cliff anywhere on the sweep.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The soft-regime budget}
Scaling one suffix block by $j$ on old inputs traces the empirical commutation residual:
relative drift $.0023\to.193$ and top-1 agreement $.997\to.434$ across $j\in[0.05,1]$,
superlinear (${\sim}j^{1.7}$) as expected under expansive composition plus LayerNorm
coupling (Figure~\ref{fig:leakage}). \emph{Protocol}: 16 EN windows; finite-difference
scaling of the ES block's encoder columns and decoder rows; reference is the prefix-only
state. Operating budget: $j\le0.15$ keeps $\ge99\%$ top-1 agreement. Hard masks remain the
only measured exact point; the theory (Corollary~\ref{cor:prefix}) says why nothing softer
can be exact.

\subsection{Consolidation pathway: merge, prune randomly, replay briefly}
This branch addresses a different question from the isolation theorem---how to consolidate
stored specialists into one fixed-width artifact---and should be read as such.
Chain-merging phase checkpoints (neuron-dimension concatenation, $\times K$ width, zero
finetuning) recovers 48--77\% of forgetting; the last-trained language pays
$+0.12$--$0.30$ nats. Recovery is retrieval, not generalization: merging only \{EN, ES\}
leaves DE forgotten while boosting included languages. Random-\emph{scatter} pruning back to
original width retains most of the benefit---magnitude-ranked pruning collapses, and
contiguous-block pruning collapses worse: within-phase knowledge is distributed, only phase
boundaries are modular. A final ${\le}15$-minute finetune on a ${\sim}9$\,MB real-token
buffer closes the remainder (Table~\ref{tab:pareto}, Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}).

At five phases the same pipeline yields a single original-width model serving all languages
at $2.55$--$2.87$ ppl (mean 1.00 nats vs.\ the endpoint's 2.13), deterministic to
$\pm0.01$ ppl across seeds$^\dagger$.

\subsection{Or skip everything: replay during training}
Mixing 20--25\% prior-language replay into each phase eliminates forgetting outright:
final EN/DE/ES $= 2.33/2.24/2.12$ against joint co-training's $2.33/2.23/2.23$, at +27\%
data and optimizer budget. Round-robin interleaving \emph{without} sufficient replay volume
does not help (EN/DE remain at 12--14 ppl): forgetting is capacity competition, not recency
decay.

\label{sec:rule}

\section{Secondary empirical observations}
\label{sec:laws}

These regularities held in every run we executed; ``law'' would overclaim their scope.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item[\textbf{O1}] Forgetting persists under frozen old parameters and is global across
parameter classes (interpretation: computational interference via the shared residual).
\item[\textbf{O2}] Weight isolation $\neq$ computation isolation; erosion accumulates per
added block through the depth-recurrent residual.
\item[\textbf{O3}] Within-phase knowledge is distributed (random beats ranked pruning;
coherent-region removal collapses); between-phase knowledge is modular (concatenation
retrieves).
\item[\textbf{O4}] In our tested regime, activation sparsity at fixed capacity is strongly
associated with data composition: flat in volume (30--90\,MB), rising ${\sim}0.5$\,pp per
added language, falling ${\sim}4$\,pp on first register mixing then saturating
(Figure~\ref{fig:grid}). Across capacity scales the capacity-to-data ratio associates
strongly with sparsity (the 25M model reaches 97.4\% on the same corpus family versus
${\sim}94\%$ at 100M).
\item[\textbf{O5}] Cross-corpus loss comparisons are void; measurement requires protocol
congruence, calibrated graph thresholds, and within-corpus framing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
Byte-level vocabularies sidestep embedding/head coupling that subword tokenization would
introduce. All results are 100M-scale on related-corpora sequences. The gate-distillation
probe tested one restricted class (linear, per-token, post-hoc); richer classes may shift
the learnability verdict. Replay uses real tokens; synthetic replay is untested. The erosion
slope is measured to five phases. The expansiveness measurement is a directional proxy over
sampled states, not an operator-norm computation.

\section{Conclusion}
Depth-recurrent weight sharing makes continual learning \emph{harder}: freezing weights does
not freeze computation, so every protection-style shortcut is excluded---in our setting,
provably so for soft mechanisms. But the same architecture's additive growth manufactures
the two properties the exact-isolation criterion demands (invariance and restriction
equivalence), reducing lifelong learning to selection plus optional consolidation. The
question the \BDH{} paper defers---how fast synaptic state becomes durable change without
catastrophe---receives here a structural answer: do not look for one plasticity rule that
both learns and remembers; build architectures where learning and remembering live in
separable components, and measure the interfaces between them.

\section{Proofs and formal details}\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Separability facts}
Throughout, (S1) \emph{coordinate separability}: encoder columns, per-neuron attention, and
decoder rows act independently per neuron, so zeroing a neuron's columns and rows removes
its contribution exactly; (S2) \emph{LayerNorm coupling}: $\operatorname{LN}$ mixes
statistics globally over $d$, and is the sole cross-neuron operation. Both verified against
the implementation (masked-forward reproduction tests).

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:dissoc}}
Take any specialist $F_A$ and define $F'(h)=F_A(h)+c\mathbf{1}$ for $c\neq0$, where the
constant is produced by a suffix module with frozen (nonzero) parameters and $\theta_A$
unchanged inside $F'$. Then $\Delta\theta_A=0$ while every old-task trajectory shifts by
$c$ per level: $F'^L(x)=F_A^L(x)+Lc\mathbf{1}\neq F_A^L(x)$. Conversely, weight isolation
places no lower bound on divergence either (take $c=0$ with divergent suffix dynamics
elsewhere). Hence the two notions are logically independent. \hfill$\qed$

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}}
($\Leftarrow$) Induction over $\ell$. Base: $h_0=E(x)\in H_A$ by assumption and
$h_0=F_A^0(x)$. Step: suppose $h_\ell=F_A^\ell(x)\in H_A\cap\TA$. By (C1),
$F'(h_\ell)\in H_A$; by (C2), its relevant components equal $F_A(h_\ell)$, i.e.
$F'(h_\ell)=F_A(h_\ell)$ as elements of $H_A$ (identifying $F_A$'s image with $H_A$).
Thus $h_{\ell+1}=F'(h_\ell)=F_A(h_\ell)=F_A^{\ell+1}(x)$.
($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $F'^L(x)=F_A^L(x)$ for all $x\in\Xa$ and all $L$, but there exists
$z^*\in\TA$ with $F'(z^*)\neq F_A(z^*)$. Choose $x\in\Xa$ whose trajectory passes through
$z^*$ at depth $\ell^*<L$ (such $x$ exists by definition of $\TA$). Applying the hypothesis
at $L=\ell^*+1$ gives $F'(z^*)=F_A(z^*)$, contradicting the assumption. Hence equality must
hold on all reachable old-trajectory states. \hfill$\qed$

\emph{Structural conditions.} If (C1) $(I-P_A)F'P_A=0$ then $F'(H_A)\subseteq H_A$: any
$z=P_Az$ has $F'(z)=P_AF'(z)+(I-P_A)F'(z)$ and $(I-P_A)F'(z)=(I-P_A)F'P_Az=0$. If moreover
(C2) $P_AF'P_A=P_AF_AP_A$ then on $H_A$, $P_AF'=P_AF_A$, i.e.\ the projected dynamics agree
with the specialist's. Together they satisfy the criterion restricted to $H_A$; combined
with $E(\Xa)\subseteq H_A$ they give the full statement. Neither alone suffices: (C1)
without (C2) confines but alters (e.g.\ $F'=2\,\mathrm{id}$, $P_A=I$: commuting, invariant,
not preserving); (C2) without (C1) matches the specialist today but admits later leakage.

\subsection{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:zf}}
With suffix gating $f_b\mapsto g_b(x)\odot f_b(b)$-terms only, the gated update is
$h_{\ell+1}=h_\ell+f(h_\ell;\theta_A)+\sum_b g_b(x)\odot f_b(h_\ell)$. Preservation
requires the sum term to vanish identically along preserved trajectories; any coordinate
with a nonzero $f_b$ component pins $g_{b,i}(x)=0$. Union over levels and inputs gives the
claim. The specialist term carries no multiplier, so no complementary constraint arises.
\hfill$\qed$

\subsection{Proof sketch of Proposition~\ref{prop:soft}}
Two-dimensional counterexample, $I_A=\{1\}$, $I_B=\{2\}$, $\sigma=\mathrm{ReLU}$:
$f'_1(x)=\sigma(x_1)+\varepsilon x_2$, $f'_2(x)=\delta\sigma(x_1)+\gamma x_2$,
$\varepsilon\delta\neq0$. Preservation from $x_0=(a,0)$, $a>0$, requires after one step
$x_1=(a(1{+}g_1),\,g_2\delta a)$ and after two
$a(1{+}g_1)+g_1\sigma(a(1{+}g_1))+g_1\varepsilon g_2\delta a=2a$ for \emph{all} $a>0$.
Substituting the first equation's constraint ($g_2=0$ or $\varepsilon\delta=0$ to kill the
cross term at unit scale) shows no open-range pair works for all $a$; with $g_2>0$ the
residual term $g_1\varepsilon\delta a g_2$ varies with $a$ while $\sigma(a(1{+}g_1))$ is
affine in $a$ only piecewise, so the identity cannot hold across $a$ crossing the kink.
Affine $f'$ removes the kink and is solvable; nonlinearity is exactly what forces hardness.
\hfill$\qed$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:amp}}
Standard recursion: with $e_{\ell+1}=e_\ell+[f'(\tilde h_\ell)-f'(h_\ell)]+\delta_\ell$ and
$\|f'(\tilde h)-f'(h)\|\le L_f\|e_\ell\|$,
$\|e_{\ell+1}\|\le(1+L_f)\|e_\ell\|+\delta_\ell$; unrolling gives the stated bound.
Contractivity $\rho<1$ on the interference subspace replaces $(1+L_f)$ and yields
$\|e_L\|\le\delta_{\max}/(1-\rho)$. \hfill$\qed$

\subsection{Remark on the measurement (Remark~\ref{rem:exp})}
Directional power iteration (6 steps, 8 old-input states, random init) estimates the
dominant singular direction's gain per composed level map; values are lower-bound-flavored
proxies, LayerNorm induces contractive directions (minima ${\approx}0.9$), and sampled
states cover held-out parliamentary English. The conclusion drawn is deliberately modest:
contraction is unsupported for these models, so contraction-based bounding is unavailable
\emph{here}; no universal impossibility is claimed.

\section{Negative-results register}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item Write-gating (importance protection): null at $\alpha\in\{0.5,0.9\}$; importance
spread thin; drift audit global (N3).
\item Constant-LR schedules: worse on acquisition and retention (N2).
\item Magnitude-ranked pruning: collapses relative to random at equal keep (15--16 vs
6.5--7.2 ppl at 25\% keep).
\item Block-structured pruning: collapses relative to scattered removal at equal keep.
\item Learned in-pass linear gates: collapse open under KD distillation; language identity
is not linearly extractable from per-token deep residuals.
\item Round-robin interleaving without replay volume: retention unchanged vs floor.
\item Top-$k$ as consolidation aid: slightly worse forgetting than ReLU at identical
schedules.
\end{enumerate}
