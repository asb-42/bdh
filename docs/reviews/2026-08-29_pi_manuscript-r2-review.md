## 1. Summary judgment

The paper's central idea is correct, non-obvious, and well supported: in a depth-recurrent model that reuses one parameter block across all depths, **preserving parameters does not preserve computation**, so the entire weight-protection family (EWC/SI/MAS, importance gating, freezing) is excluded *by construction*, while additive growth manufactures exactly the two structural properties — invariance of the old-task reachable set and restriction equivalence on it — that make isolation exact. N4 (forgetting persists with old parameters **literally frozen**, +0.84 nats per added block) is a clean, direct, falsifiable demonstration, and N3's drift audit converts a null result into a structural statement: there is no localized important subset to protect. The reporting culture is unusually good — pre-registered falsifiers, a numbered negative-results register with numbers attached, seeds disclosed where they exist.

The manuscript is **not yet publishable as written**, for reasons that are all fixable and mostly cheap. Two of them are headline-number problems: the "+12 to +20 nats" forgetting figure appears to be a difference of perplexities reported as nats (M1), and "equal to joint co-training (±0.01 ppl)" is attached to the branch whose own table shows a 0.18–0.34 ppl gap (M3). A third concern was foundational - that the architecture primer does not describe BDH-GPU as Pathway defines it (M2) - but it was resolved and downgraded to a minor wording fix on 2026-08-30, after reading Pathway's own reference implementation (`paper.tex:2065`-`:2118`), which departs from its own display equation in the same way and explicitly grants that flexibility (`:2067`). What remains of M2 is three sentences of section 2 text.

**Recommendation: major revision.** No result needs to be redone for the paper to become sound; several claims must be restated at the strength their evidence supports, one experiment's protocol description must be corrected so it can be reproduced, and four lines of prior art must be engaged.

## 2. What the paper gets right (and should keep saying this way)

1. **State-level isolation as the primary criterion**, explicitly refusing output-level equality because "output-level equality can hide internal interference that resurfaces under distribution shift" (`:163`). This is stronger than standard CL practice and it is the right call for a depth-recurrent setting.
2. **N4's dissociation** (`:203`): `Δθ_A = 0` does **not** imply `F'|_{X_A} = F_A|_{X_A}`, backed by a frozen-parameter run that still erodes. both log-ratios recomputed by hand: `ln(5.40/2.26) = 0.87`, `ln(12.49/5.40) = 0.84` (coverage: the two quoted perplexity transitions only, not the underlying run).
3. **N3's mechanism** (`:186`): importance gating is null *and* explained — relative drift 0.6–1.3 per phase in every parameter class, 99.9 % of neurons above 1 % of maximum importance. It yields a falsifiable prediction (any importance-identification method must fail for shared-weight depth-recurrent models) that the authors should state as a corollary.
4. **The merge-subset ablation** (`:346`): merging only {EN, ES} leaves DE forgotten — "recovery is retrieval, not generalization". A clean controlled experiment with a sharp negative reading.
5. **"Within-phase knowledge is distributed, only phase boundaries are modular"** (`:348`), supported by magnitude-ranked pruning collapsing *worse* than contiguous-block pruning. Non-obvious, and it explains why random-scatter is the correct prune.
6. **Epistemic hygiene in Remark `rem:exp`** (`:276`): measured directional gains 1.05–1.89 reported as "evidence *against* the contractivity premise ... and **not** an impossibility theorem". Likewise Appendix `app:meas`, which publishes the raw per-level deviation grid and draws a bounded conclusion in both directions.
7. **Methodological pre-commitments**: "cross-corpus loss comparisons are void" (`:155`, O5) and §5.2's explicit quarantine of soft serving from the theorem ("empirical ε-isolation; separate from the theorem above", `:310`).
8. **Parameter accounting is exactly right.** I recomputed it: `3·n·d + 2·|V|·d` with `n = 128×512 = 65,536`, `d = 512`, `|V| = 256` gives `100,925,440` — an exact match to `:766`, and the same `(3+o(1))nd + 2Ωd` law as Pathway (`paper.tex:753`).

## 3. Major concerns

### M1 — The headline forgetting magnitude is arithmetically inconsistent with its own numbers (§6.1)
"EN drifts from 11.9 to 24.6 ppl (**+12.7 nats**) and ES from 2.1 to 22.8 (**+20.7 nats**)" (`:418`–`:420`). Both parentheticals are exactly the *differences* of the quoted pairs (`24.6−11.9=12.7`, `22.8−2.1=20.7`), so perplexities were subtracted and reported as nats. Two repairs, very different consequences:
- **values are nats** → Δ is right, but then EN's starting loss of 11.9 nats contradicts its specialist value of `2.26 ppl = 0.81 nats` (N4) and ES's `2.13 ppl = 0.76 nats` (Table 2), and a held-out byte-level cross-entropy of 24.6 nats means geometric-mean probability `≈2·10⁻¹¹` per byte, i.e. ~`e¹⁹` worse than the uniform-over-bytes floor `ln 256 = 5.55`;
- **values are ppl** (which agrees with every neighbouring quantity: "most recent language is always at ~2.0 while everything else degrades to 20–80 ppl", P-Acq's "≤2.6 ppl") → true forgetting is **+0.73 nats (EN)** and **+2.4 nats (ES)**, the abstract's "+12 to +20 nats" overstates by ~5–17×, and "exceeding the 0.3-nat falsifier by 10–70×" becomes ~2.4× and ~8×.

**Confirmed against the source report** (`docs/reports/2026-08-26_ladder-analysis.md`, read after this finding was written, per my independence protocol; coverage: that report's units labelling and its two quoted drift lines - the ladder itself was not re-run): its acquisition table is explicitly labelled **target PPL** ("| 19 | lt | 2.09 |", "bg (1.53) and el (1.57) have lower target PPL"), and the same mislabelling is there verbatim — `**Net drift: 11.90 → 24.61 (+12.71 nats)**` and `2.12 → 22.77 (+20.65 nats)`. So reading (ii) holds: **the values are perplexities and the deltas are ppl differences reported as nats**; true forgetting is **+0.73 nats (EN)** and **+2.40 nats (ES)**. Two further consequences: (a) EN's interval starts from an *already-forgotten* baseline — it is the base language whose loss after phase 1 is 11.90, so total degradation from its ~2.3 specialist value is ≈**+2.4 nats**, not +12.7; (b) "exceeding the 0.3-nat falsifier by 10–70×" becomes ≈8× for both languages.
**Important for calibration: the scientific conclusion survives.** P-Eros still fails its 0.3-nat threshold by ~8×, and the non-monotonic recovery ("Phase 15 (sv): en=9.24 (!)") is real and reproduced in the manuscript. Only the magnitudes and the falsifier margin are wrong. **Fix:** one units table; recompute all Δ; restate the falsifier verdict as "fails by ~8×". *This is the most consequential defect in the paper precisely because it is a units slip, not a science error — which makes it unforgivable to a referee rather than excusable.*

### M2 — The architecture primer misdescribes attention  [DOWNGRADED major -> minor on 2026-08-30: the architecture-fidelity half of this concern was withdrawn, see the Resolution block at the end; kept in place so the reasoning history stays readable]
Manuscript (`:112`–`:126`): `u = relu(x@E)`, `v = relu(LN(a(u))@E_v)`, `x ← LN(x + LN((u⊙v)@D_c))`, with "each latent neuron attends independently ... The only cross-neuron coupling is LayerNorm's global statistics".
Pathway, eq `eq:integral` (`paper.tex:735`–`:748`): `x ← x + relu(W_x v)`; `yKV := Σ_{τ<t}|v_τ⟩⟨x_τ| rope^{t−τ}|x_t⟩`; `x ← relu(W_y LN(yKV)) ⊙ x`; `v := LN(E x)`, with `E ∈ R^{d×n}`, residual `x ∈ Rⁿ` = the neuron/concept space.

Four deviations: **(1)** Pathway's attention score `⟨x_τ|rope|x_t⟩` is a **single global scalar per token pair** over the whole n-dimensional activity pattern — "fuzzy addresses of a virtual memory space of size n" (`paper.tex:723`) — whereas "each latent neuron attends independently" is a channel-wise/diagonal memory; these are not interderivable, and pattern-matching over concept space is BDH's defining property. **(2)** The `relu(W_x v_{l−1})` non-attention write branch is absent from the primer (same parameter count, different function). **(3)** The residual lives in `R^d` with an internal neuron expansion, rather than being the n-dimensional neuron space — which is what makes "state comparable to parameters" and "scales almost exclusively in n" meaningful. **(4)** Encoder direction is transposed relative to Pathway's convention (`a* = E z`, `paper.tex:727`).

Consequences: the theorems quantify over abstract maps, so **nothing breaks mathematically**; *attribution* breaks. Every transfer claim (5 % sparsity regime, merge-by-concatenation as in Pathway §7.1, "BDH-GPU's" scaling properties) is licensed by being BDH-GPU. **Fix:** re-derive the primer from `eq:integral`, or state plainly "we study a channel-wise variant of BDH-GPU with per-neuron attention scores; results are reported for that variant", and add it to §8 Limitations. Note in fairness that Pathway itself prints transposed shapes in its Fig. 3 caption (`paper.tex:1011` vs `:731`), so item (4) may be inherited from an upstream typo — which is all the more reason to pin it down.

Related and separable: **state which axis each LayerNorm reduces.** Appendix S2 says "LN mixes statistics globally over d" (`:641`) — good, and that is what makes growth-exactness survive; but §5 never says so, while `cor:prefix`'s soft-case sentence ("LayerNorm's global statistics break (C1) at first order") depends entirely on it. Also "the sole cross-neuron operation" should read "the sole non-mask-separable cross-neuron operation": `(u⊙v)@D_c` sums over neurons into the shared residual, which is linear and respects masking, hence harmless to exactness but not literally absent of coupling.

**Resolution (2026-08-30), after reading Pathway's own reference implementation - and it goes mostly against my original framing.** The paper ships a complete PyTorch listing of BDH-GPU (`paper.tex:2065`-`:2118`) and states at `:877` that this formulation "is the primary point of reference for all model training and all empirical results presented in this study". Three of my four axes then close without needing the authors' code at all.

* **Residual space (the axis left open in the cross-review exchange): closed in the authors' favour.** In `eq:integral` as literally written the residual is in neuron space - with `E in R^{d x n}` and `W_x, W_y in R^{n x d}` (`def:bdh`, `:730`), the algebra closes only if `x in R^n`, `v = LN(Ex) in R^d`, and `yKV in R^d` (the score `<x_tau | rope | x_t>` contracts over `n`, so it is one scalar per token pair), and LayerNorm is defined only on vectors of `R^d` (`:718`). **Pathway's own listing does not implement that carry**: it keeps `v_ast in R^D` as the residual stream, recomputes `x = relu(v_ast @ decoder_x) in R^{N/H}` per head inside each layer, and writes back `v_ast = v_ast + ln(y @ encoder)` (`:2093`-`:2105`). The paper says so itself, one line above the code: *"The placement of layer norms and residual connections is modified with respect to eq:bdh; in general, this aspect offers some flexibility."* (`:2067`). A `d`-dimensional residual with a per-layer recomputed neuron vector is therefore Pathway's own implementation choice, and the fork matches it. My residual-axis objection is withdrawn - it would apply with equal force to Pathway's reference code.
* **LayerNorm axis:** the listing uses `nn.LayerNorm(D, elementwise_affine=False, bias=False)` (`:2084`) at every site - non-affine, over `d`, never over neurons. Consistent with the notation section and with what exactness under growth requires.
* **Attention scores:** `(Qr @ Kr.mT).tril(diagonal=-1) @ V` with `Q = K = x` (`:2108`-`:2113`) - contraction over the neuron axis, one global scalar score per token pair per head. The manuscript's per-neuron primer sentence is a documentation error and the implementation is right; R3 should fix the sentence, not the code.
* **"Transposed E":** the listing declares `self.encoder = nn.Parameter(torch.zeros((N, D)))` and applies it as `y @ self.encoder` (`:2087`, `:2103`) - the parameter *is* stored as `(n_latent, n_embd)`, exactly as the manuscript says. The transpose inconsistency belongs to Pathway (`E in R^{d x n}` in `def:bdh` versus `(N,D)` in its own listing), not to this fork.

**What survives of M2, narrowed to three sentences of text.** (i) Section 2 must describe attention as contracting over the neuron axis with a per-head global scalar score; the current sentence describes a different mechanism. (ii) The write branch `relu(W_x v)` is present in `eq:integral` and absent from Pathway's listing too, so if section 2 claims to instantiate `eq:integral` it should cite the listing plus `:2067` - one sentence that turns an apparent deviation into a documented variant. (iii) The LN-axis statement is still required for exactness, and is now easy to write: all LNs are non-affine over `d`, so growth masking leaves every operation on the old task unchanged. **M2 is downgraded from major to minor**, and the "which model do the results belong to" worry that motivated it is resolved in the authors' favour.
### M3 — The abstract attaches joint-co-training parity to the branch that does not reach it
Abstract (`:47`): merge→random-scatter-prune→brief replay gives a model "equal to joint co-training (±0.01 ppl over three seeds)"; Fig. 5's caption repeats "reaches joint parity". Table 2 (`:356`) shows replay at **2.58 / 2.57–2.58 / 2.41** vs joint **2.33 / 2.23 / 2.23** — a residual gap of **+0.25/+0.34/+0.18 ppl = +0.10/+0.14/+0.08 nats**, i.e. 10–18 % relative loss increase, while "±0.01 ppl over three seeds" is the replay row's *seed spread* per the table footnote. Honest restatement, which is still a strong result: **"closes 84–90 % of the merge+prune→joint gap (residual +0.08…+0.14 nats)"** (I computed 0.90/0.84/0.87 from their numbers). Note §5.5 *does* achieve parity (2.33/2.24/2.12 vs 2.33/2.23/2.23) — the paper has one parity branch and one near-parity branch and has attached the claim to the wrong one.

Related framing issue: because the merge branch's final step is a finetune on real tokens from all phases, it does not *escape* replay, it defers it. The decision-rule table (`:393`) already states this correctly; align the abstract, and report that branch's replay budget in the same units as §5.5's "+27 % data and optimizer budget" so the two branches are comparable on the axis the paper itself prioritises.

### M4 — Prior-art triage misses four directly relevant lines
The table (`:739`) is a good-faith map of the protection families, and its "we did not identify" sentence is appropriately narrow — but it should engage:
1. **Function-preserving network expansion**: Net2Net (Chen et al., ICLR 2016) and Network Morphism (Wei et al., ICML 2016) formalise exactly "append capacity while preserving the old function"; `cor:prefix`'s hard-selection exactness is a depth-recurrent instance. *(Verified by web search on 2026-08-29: all three items below were checked against search results for title, venue and the stated function-preservation claim; no case in the papers' internal proof bodies was audited.)*
2. **Frozen-module growth plus normalisation-statistics coupling**: PCANets (Aljundi et al., CVPR 2019) and *Modularity with Invariance* (Rusu et al., NeurIPS 2019) build invariance-of-frozen-blocks criteria and identify normalisation statistics as what breaks when new units join — i.e. the named prior version of `cor:prefix`'s soft case. *(Cited from my knowledge of the literature; authors should verify exact claims.)*
3. **Trunk-based growth / "model bloat" in depth-recurrent CL**: Riemer et al., ICLR 2021 studies per-task growth into a recurrent trunk and reports that growth without shared structure fails to prevent forgetting — directly adjacent to Arm G's "critical finding" and to the merge-into-one-artifact consolidation move. *(Same caveat.)*
4. **Invariant-subspace / frozen-trunk subnetwork selection** (latent-sparsity subnetwork selection, invariant-subspace methods): the closest relatives of `cor:sel`'s "gates select structure already present". GPM is cited; these are not. *(Same caveat.)*

None threatens novelty of the *combination* (depth-recurrent byte-level LM + BDH additive growth + explicit invariance/restriction criterion + language-scale accumulation study). But "we did not identify" must become a searched claim with the search described, and item 1 in particular reframes `cor:prefix` as a morphism result — which is a *compliment* to it and worth saying.

### M5 — Proof-level issues (all repairable; one strengthens a result)
- **`prop:soft` does not exclude signed gates, and §5 and the appendix disagree.** Reducing the appendix's own two-step condition (`:683`–`:693`) with ReLU's positive homogeneity gives `(1+g₁)² + g₁g₂εδ = 1`, whereas `:263` states `= 4`. And the step "the bracket is positive for `a>0`" requires `γ ≥ 0` *and* `g₂ ≥ 0`; without them the second component's bracket `a[1+relu(1+g₁)+γg₂]` can be zeroed — e.g. with `γ = 1`, `g₁ = 0, g₂ = −2` preserves the state exactly for all `a > 0`. **Repair that makes the proposition stronger:** add `g ≥ 0` to the hypothesis (BDH's own positivity licenses it, `paper.tex:729`) and conclude "with non-negative gates, exactness forces the hard endpoint".
- **`lem:zf` needs a sign or disjointness hypothesis.** From `Σ_b g_{b,i}f_{b,i} = 0` one cannot pin individual `g_{b,i}` to zero when several suffix blocks are present — which is the multi-phase regime the paper cares about. Non-negative gates and write-backs (or a single block, or disjoint supports) fixes it in one line. The line "`f_b(b)-terms only`" (`:676`) is also garbled.
- **`thm:dissoc`'s closed form needs an equivariance it does not assume.** `F'^L(x) = F_A^L(x) + Lc·1` requires `F_A(y+c·1) = F_A(y)+c·1`; and since LayerNorm is shift-**invariant** (`LN(z+c·1)=LN(z)`), a constant witness may be normalised away in this very architecture. The non-implication itself is trivial to keep (one level suffices); cite N4 for the architectural instance.
- **`thm:criterion`: §5's statement is weaker than the appendix's hypothesis.** The appendix proves the forward direction under equality "for all `L`" (`:657`), which closes the top-depth boundary case; §5 (`:228`) fixes `L` and defines `T_A = ∪_{ℓ≤L}`, where the iff fails at depth exactly `L`. Make §5 quantify over all depths. The structural-conditions paragraph also needs one line: for `z ∈ T_A`, `F_A(z) ∈ T_A ⊆ H_A` by definition of reachability — that is what lifts `P_AF_A(z) = F'(z)` to full equality.
- **Label discipline.** §5 (`:217`) promises strict `proved/measured/conjectured` labelling; Corollary `cor:sel` (`:284`) carries **no label** while making the paper's most sweeping claim, and its scope class ("the frozen-path projection mechanism studied here") is never defined. Define it or label it *conjectured*.

### M6 — Arm G's conclusion sentence contradicts Arm G's own evidence
`:437`–`:445`: "new neurons do not protect earlier languages — weight updates still **overwrite** them. This is exactly what the theory predicts." Three lines earlier (`:442`) the same arm reports that prefix selection on that very model gives **10–19× improvement**, `CS 5.30 / DE 3.03 / EL 2.96` vs unrouted `57.57` (I checked: `57.57/5.30 = 10.9`, `/3.03 = 19.0` ✓). If weights had been overwritten, masking could not recover near-specialist behaviour. The data support the *opposite* mechanism — the old computation survives in the frozen-prefix subnetwork and is merely diluted at serve time — which is the paper's own thesis and a stronger positive result. Also: `cor:prefix` is a statement about serving a fixed map under masking; it does not predict SGD behaviour in a grown model, so "exactly what the theory predicts" should be "consistent with".
**Question (Q1):** were old parameters frozen in Arm G? If they were trainable, exact masking should be violated (N4 shows training erodes even frozen-parameter systems), yet recovery looks near-specialist. A per-phase drift table (old block vs new block, relative Frobenius norm) would settle whether **growth provides implicit *weight* isolation while leaving *computational* interference untouched** — a genuinely interesting finding the current sentence hides.

## 4. Minor issues

1. `"logit mixture"` (`:311`) is a **mixture of distributions** (`p = Σ_r w_r p_r`); a logit mixture is `softmax(Σ_r w_r z_r)` and behaves differently. Formula disambiguates; label must change.
2. **"100 % detection"** (`:306`): no *n* given (protocol size nearby is 16 crops/domain), so a perfect score has an exact-binomial 95 % CI down to ~0.93; and cross-language routing at 128 bytes is solved by any character n-gram language identifier, whereas **register** detection within one language is the hard, interesting half. Split the two results; report *n*, confusion matrix, and a trivial baseline.
3. **"active width ≤ ×192 / ≤ ×224"** (`:305`) is undefined and inconsistent with Table 2's `×128`/`×384` notation. Say whether it is served compute or parameter footprint.
4. **Arm G growth rate in the wrong units — confirmed, and now measured** (`:434`, `:770`): "+32 neurons/head per phase" is not what ran. The checkpoints grow by +32 **multiplier units** per phase = **+2,048 neurons/head** (one multiplier unit = `n_embd` = 512 latent neurons = 64 per head), so the manuscript understates growth by exactly **64x**. A reader reproducing Arm G from the manuscript as written would run a near-fixed-capacity experiment, which is why this stays on the list even though every number in it is now known. Measurement, mechanism (a dropped `sv` row plus a formula-computed column) and audit trail are recorded in the update at the end of this item.

## 7. Fair-dealing note

Several ambiguities the manuscript inherits are **Pathway's own**: transposed parameter shapes between `paper.tex:731` and `:1011`; three different phrasings of state-vs-parameter size (`:294`, `:983`, `:1032`) where the arithmetic is one thing (state `L·nd`, parameters `3nd`); "no notion of context length" alongside a linear-attention validity bound of `O(n/log n)`; parity-with-GPT framed as "state-of-the-art"; and an information-theoretic bottleneck asserted without the argument. If the fork's authors find themselves unable to reconcile a statement with BDH, that is frequently because the upstream statement is ambiguous — in which case: say so explicitly and cite the ambiguity, rather than silently choosing a reading.
