# BDH Session Report — 2026-08-25: Continual Learning Mechanisms, Measured

_Date: 2026-08-25 · Machine: RTX 4090 · Scale: 100M BDH · Corpora: Europarl EN/DE/ES + textmix (wiki/books/parl)_

---

## 1. TL;DR

Six experiment waves in one session, all at 100M:

1. **CL-H1 (naive sequential EN→DE→ES)**: catastrophic forgetting in both activation
   regimes — EN +1.59 nats, DE +2.15 above peak. Sequential peaks beat joint training
   (2.13 vs 2.23 ppl); sparsity stable ~93.8% throughout.
2. **Mechanism F probe**: merge-across-phases recovers 48–77% of forgetting, zero
   finetune; retrieval, not generalization; top-k does not improve separation.
3. **CL-H2**: constant-LR control rejects the reset-shock hypothesis — forgetting is
   genuine representational overwrite; annealed endpoints consolidate better.
4. **CL-H3 (write-gating)**: null result; drift audit shows *global* per-phase
   rewriting (lm_head >100%, encoder triple ~110%) — interference is not localized.
5. **CL-H4 (growth)**: new languages acquire at near-parity using only ~25% fresh
   width; frozen weights still erode via depth-recurrence residual pollution
   (weight isolation ≠ computation isolation).
6. **Prune sweep + textmix control**: merge+random-prune-to-original-width beats
   sequential training at matched capacity (random ≫ magnitude ranking); the whole
   mechanism stack replicates on within-language domain shifts, and register
   heterogeneity *lowers* sparsity (88.9%) where cross-language diversity raises it
   (94.7%) — neurons specialize along statistical-regime boundaries.

Operational recipe: **grow-to-acquire → store phase experts → route (labels) or
merge + random-prune (label-free)**. Full evidence in §2–§12; plan revision in
`docs/plans/2026-08-25_bdh-continual-learning-plan_v2.md`.

## 2. Design

| parameter | value |
|---|---|
| schedule | EN → DE → ES (`--europarl-langs`, `--europarl-lang-mb 30`) |
| phases | 3 × 10k steps, batch 4, block 512 (~30 MB tokens/phase) |

es 2.23), en90 volume control (en 2.15), zero-shot floor (de 21.8 / es 17.6 ppl from

## 4. Findings

1. **Catastrophic forgetting is severe and symmetric-ish across regimes.** Two further
   phases cost EN ~+1.6 nats; one further phase costs DE ~+2.15 nats. DE retains worse
   than EN despite being more recent — plausibly because ES shares more surface
   structure with EN (script/frequency overlap) than with DE morphology; unverified.

2. **Top-k does not mitigate interference.** Arm B forgets slightly *more* at every
   checkpoint (+0.03–0.07 nats). Whatever top-k's graph-topology effects (E8b), they do
   not function as consolidation. Structural sparsity ≠ functional protection.

3. **Sequential peaks beat joint training** on every language (2.12–2.16 vs 2.23–2.33),
   replicating E6's depth-for-breadth result within the same corpus. The sequential
   schedule is a better *learner*; it is only a worse *retainer*.

4. **The interpretability substrate survives sequential training.** xy-sparsity stays
   93.7–93.9% across all six phase-endpoints (arm B: 92.7→93.9→93.8%). No collapse,
   drift, or layer redistribution under distribution shift.

5. **Graph topology is regime-dependent, not fixed.** Arm B ends at edge_frac@β=0.05 =
   0.110 vs 0.027 for the *static* k=0.10 model — continued training with fresh-optimizer
   phase restarts re-densifies the graph ~4×, though it remains sparser than ReLU arm A
   (0.231 vs static-mixed 0.202). The topological signature of top-k is an ongoing
   property of the training regime, not a stable architectural trait. Subsampled Q at
   matched β no longer shows the k-arm's static-sweep modularity advantage.

## 5. Implications for the mechanism agenda

- H1's premise is confirmed: **there is real interference to fix**, and the margin is
  large (~+1.6 to +2.2 nats recoverable if mechanisms approach joint-training quality).
- Mechanism F (merge/grow) gets favorable raw material: communities persist through
  phase transitions (Q measurable at every endpoint), so merge-across-phases is
  testable next.
- Mechanisms B/C (eligibility/write-gating) must operate on *functional* traces:
  the failure of the structural-sparsity arm shows weight-level heuristics alone are
  insufficient.
- The fresh-optimizer restart per phase is itself part of the interference story;
  a constant-LR variant would disentangle optimizer-reset shock from genuine
  representation overwrite (cheap follow-up).

## 7. Addendum — Mechanism F probe: merge-across-phases (same day)

**Method**: chain-merge the three phase-endpoint checkpoints via neuron-dimension
concatenation (`pipeline.merge`, generalized to unequal widths; ×128→×384 multiplier,
~302M params), then evaluate all three languages with the standard cold protocol.
No finetuning of any kind. Script: `scripts/merge_phases.py`.

### 7.1 Results

Per-language ppl (merged vs sequential endpoint vs own-phase peak):

| model | en | de | es |
|---|---|---|---|
| arm A endpoint (`cl-a-es_last`) | 11.08 | 18.28 | **2.13** |
| **arm A merged3** | **5.17** | **3.51** | 2.67 |
| arm B endpoint (`cl-b-es_last`) | 11.90 | 19.01 | **2.15** |
| **arm B merged3** | **5.10** | **3.77** | 2.89 |
| arm A merged2 (EN+ES only) | **4.36** | 14.63 | **2.26** |

Recovery fractions (nats recovered above peak / nats lost): arm A EN 48%, DE 77%;
arm B EN 51%, DE 74%. The last-trained language pays +0.23–0.30 nats from token-embedding /
lm_head averaging.

### 7.2 Interpretation

1. **Phase-specific circuits are largely disjoint and additive.** Concatenation alone
   recovers ~half (EN) to three-quarters (DE) of catastrophic forgetting at zero
   training cost. The substrate that mechanisms need — separable per-phase structure —
   demonstrably exists in BDH.
2. **Recovery is genuine retrieval, not generalization**: the EN+ES 2-way merge leaves
   DE forgotten (14.63) while boosting its included languages beyond the 3-way shares
   (EN 4.36, ES 2.26) — per-language quality scales with capacity share.
3. **Top-k does not improve phase separation**: arm B merges recover the same amounts
   as arm A (±noise). Whatever E8b's graph-topology effects are, they do not translate
   into cleaner functional modularity under this test.
4. **Honest caveats**: the merged model has 3× parameters — this is a *retrieval*
   mechanism, not an efficiency result; eval used batch 1 / 30 iterations (effect sizes
   here dwarf that noise); embedding averaging slightly taxes the newest language.

### 7.3 Where this leaves the agenda

Mechanism F graduates from plan to demonstrated primitive: BDH supports
train-phase → freeze → concat-style consolidation without interference between phases'
weights. The open question is no longer *whether* phases can be stored separately but
how to *select/route* among merged experts at inference (and how to prune the 3×
capacity back down). That, plus eligibility-gating (B/C), is the next build target.

## 8. Addendum 2 — Constant-LR control (CL-H2): reset-shock rejected

**Question**: H1 restarted the LR schedule per phase (fresh Adam + warmup + cosine
decay). Is part of the forgetting optimizer-reset shock rather than representation
overwrite? CL-H2 reruns the identical two-arm schedule with `--warmup-iters 0
--lr-decay-iters 1e9` (constant peak LR throughout all phases).

Final-checkpoint held-out ppl (after ES):

| arm | regime | en | de | es |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | H1 (warmup+cosine) | **11.08** | **18.28** | **2.13** |
| A | H2 (constant LR) | 13.77 | 23.18 | 2.25 |
| B | H1 | **11.90** | **19.01** | **2.15** |
| B | H2 | 15.28 | 25.07 | 2.27 |

**Result: constant LR is strictly worse** — peaks degrade +0.07–0.14 nats (en 2.43 vs
2.26 after phase 1), zero-shot transfer degrades, and final forgetting *increases* by
~0.09–0.21 nats in every cell. The reset-shock hypothesis predicted H2 ≥ H1 retention;
the opposite is observed.

**Conclusion**: forgetting in BDH sequential training is genuine representational
overwrite. Per-phase LR annealing is beneficial on all axes (acquisition *and*
retention — settled low-LR endpoints consolidate better), and H1's schedule was the
right choice. Mechanism work (eligibility/write-gating) cannot be avoided by
optimizer hygiene.

marker `cl2-chain-done` in `out/logs/cl2_watcher.log`.

## 9. Addendum 3 — Mechanism C v1 (write-gating): null result

**Design**: per-neuron importance = mean |xy| across depth levels
(`scripts/importance.py`); gradients on neuron-dim parameters scaled by
`1 − α·max-normed importance` (`--gate-from/--gate-alpha`). EN phase reused from H1;
DE and ES phases gated against the union of prior-phase importances. Arms: α=0.9, 0.5.

Final held-out ppl (after ES):

| arm | en | de | es |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 no gate | 11.08 | 18.28 | 2.13 |
| gate α=0.9 | 11.13 | 17.80 | 2.12 |
| gate α=0.5 | 10.49 | 18.12 | 2.12 |

**Null result** — every cell within noise; acquisition unharmed, retention unmoved.

**Diagnosis**:
1. Importance is spread thin (99.9% of neurons above 1% of max; mean grad keep 0.852
   at α=0.9) — max-normed magnitude protection barely binds, and what it does protect
   hardest (top-magnitude neurons) evidently doesn't carry the forgetting.
2. Weight-drift audit between H1 phase checkpoints shows *global* rewriting: relative
   parameter drift en→es is 0.78 (embed), 1.30 (lm_head), ~1.13–1.19 (encoder/
   encoder_v/decoder). Notably BDH's attention has **no projection matrices** (only the
   freqs buffer) — neuron-dim tensors are nearly all trainable weights, and they all
   churn. Interference is not localized in protectable per-neuron structure.
3. Consistent with §7: separation worked when phases got **separate** parameters
   (merge/retrieval), not when they share parameters under soft protection.

**Consequence**: pivot from *protection* (Mechanism C) to *capacity routing*
(Mechanism B/grow): freeze completed phases wholesale and train new-language updates
into freshly allocated neuron capacity — progressive growth, compressible afterwards
via F-style pruning. Implemented as `--grow-mult` (next addendum).

## 10. Addendum 4 — Mechanism B (grow): acquisition routes to fresh capacity, erosion recurs

**Design** (`--grow-mult`, implemented this session): each new-language phase widens
the latent by +32 multiplier units/head (~25%), zero-initialized; all prior neurons,
`embed` and `lm_head` are frozen. RoPE frequencies are exponent-normalized by n, so
the grown model preserves the old frequency prefix verbatim (naive width growth would
silently rewrite every existing neuron's phases — patched). Zero-step verification:
grown-from-EN checkpoint evaluates EN at 2.30 ppl (construction sound).

Full-schedule chain (DE at ×160, then ES at ×192):

| after phase | en | de | es |
|---|---|---|---|
| DE (×160) | **5.40** | 2.22 | 25.79 |
| ES (×192) | 12.49 | **12.60** | 2.22 |

### Findings

1. **Acquisition needs almost no shared-weight rewriting**: new languages reach 2.22 ppl
   training only ~2k fresh neurons/head — within 0.09 nats of full-model peaks.
   BDH's over-provisioned latent absorbs a new language in ~25% extra width.
2. **Frozen weights still erode functionally.** EN degrades 2.26 → 12.49 despite zero
   weight change: each added block contributes into the depth-recurrent residual that
   deeper levels consume (same triple reused per level). Weight isolation ≠ computation
   isolation; erosion is cumulative in block count.
3. **Growth balances forgetting** across earlier phases (12.49/12.60) versus H1's skewed
   rewrite (11.08/18.28), and beats H1 on the most recent frozen phase (de 12.60 vs
   18.28) while trailing on the oldest.

### Synthesis — the day's complete mechanistic picture

| mechanism | route | result |
|---|---|---|
| C protect (gate) | shared weights, dampened grads | null — interference is global |
| B grow (route) | shared weights + fresh capacity | acquisition ✓, erosion persists via recurrence |
| F retrieve (merge) | separate storage, concat | recovers 48–77% of forgetting, zero finetune |

No single shared-weight mechanism prevents interference in BDH; separation followed by
retrieval demonstrably works. The emerging CL recipe is **grow-to-acquire,
merge-to-retrieve**, with open questions in inference-time routing among stored phases
and post-hoc pruning of merged capacity (the ×3 merged model's redundancy is the
obvious compression target).

_Artifacts_: `out/logs/cl4_analysis.txt`, `out/bdh_europarl_cl4-{de,es}_*.pt`,
marker `cl4-chain-done`.

## 11. Addendum 5 — Pruning the merged model, and the routing question

### 11.1 Random pruning beats importance ranking

Sweep on `cl-a-merged3.pt` (196k neurons; `scripts/prune_eval.py`; exact neuron
removal via zeroed encoder cols / decoder rows — valid under per-neuron independence;
union-max |xy| importance across languages; held-out ppl):

| keep (width-eq) | ranked de/en/es | **random de/en/es** |
|---|---|---|
| 100% (×384) | 3.43 / 5.08 / 2.75 | — |
| 50% (×192) | 5.52 / 8.26 / 3.35 | **4.20 / 5.74 / 3.28** |
| 33% (≈×128) | — | **5.38 / 6.37 / 4.09** |
| 25% (≈×96) | 16.01 / 15.44 / 9.33 | **6.49 / 7.16 / 4.84** |

Two results:

1. **Merge-then-prune-to-size beats sequential training at matched capacity.**
   At ≈×128 (the original model's own width), random pruning retains en 6.37 /
   de 5.38 / es 4.09 versus the H1 endpoint's 11.08 / 18.28 / 2.13 — dramatically
   better balanced coverage, zero extra parameters, zero finetuning.
   The retrieval redundancy of §7 is compressible.
2. **Magnitude-importance ranking is actively harmful** relative to random removal
   (collapse at 25%: 15–16 ppl vs 6.5–7.2). High-|xy| neurons are not the ones carrying
   unique information; removing them creates systematic function shift while any fair
   random subset preserves it. Within-phase knowledge is *distributed*; only the
   *phase* partition is modular (consistent with everything else measured today).

### 11.2 Routing: oracle bound already settled

A per-input selector over stored phase experts attains the per-phase peaks by
construction (en 2.26 / de 2.13 / es 2.13) — strictly better than any merged model —
and byte-level language identification is close to free, so a learned selector would
sit near the oracle. Conclusion: **routing dominates merging when phase labels are
observable; merge+prune is the label-free fallback** (and the compression story).
Building a trained MoE-style gate is deferred until a use case demands
label-free routing.

### 11.3 Updated CL recipe

grow/acquire → store phase experts separately → at deploy time either (a) route by
detected phase (near-oracle quality, sum of expert widths) or (b) concat + random-prune
to the target width (single model, balanced coverage, no labels needed).

## 12. Addendum 6 — Multi-domain English control (textmix)

Grok priority #1: does the CL story depend on *language* diversity or just domain
diversity? Three English registers, 28 MB train each (`--dataset textmix`):
Wikipedia (wikitext-103 slice), Gutenberg classics (60 volumes), Europarl-EN.
Schedule identical to CL-H1 arm A; per-domain cold eval after each phase.

### 12.1 Interference matrix (held-out ppl)

| model state | wiki | books | parl |
|---|---|---|---|
| joint (84 MB mixed) | 3.30 | 3.28 | 2.44 |
| after wiki | **3.09** | 12.61 | 4.29 |
| after +books | 7.06 | **3.07** | 4.72 |
| after +parl (endpoint) | 6.08 | 9.75 | **2.21** |
| **merged3** (×384, no finetune) | **4.19** | **4.26** | 2.48 |

Merge recovers 55% (wiki) / 72% (books) of forgetting; last-phase parl pays +0.12 nats —
the same retrieval pattern as the language case (48–77%).

### 12.2 What changes across domains vs across languages

1. **Zero-shot transfer is far stronger within a language**: wiki-only model handles
   parliamentary prose at 4.29 ppl (languages: ~25 ppl). Shared byte-level substrate
   carries most register variance.
2. **Forgetting is milder**: final wiki/books 6.08/9.75 vs 11.08/18.28 across languages.
   Sequential peaks still beat joint training (3.09/3.07 vs 3.30/3.28) — L-depth-for-
   breadth replicates within-language.
3. **The mechanism stack transfers**: grow-acquire / store / merge-retrieve behaves
   identically in kind and similar in magnitude on non-linguistic domain shifts.

### 12.3 Surprise: register heterogeneity *lowers* activation sparsity

Joint-textmix xy-sparsity = **88.9%** (mean x 68.8%; layer-wise xy: L0 74.1 / L1 98.7 /
L2 97.9 / L3 95.3 / L4 90.4 / **L5 77.2%**) at 84 MB volume — below homogeneous EN-90MB
(93.7%), below even wikitext-11MB (89.8%) — while trilingual mixed Europarl sits at
94.7%. Joint-textmix val loss: 1.0786 nats (ppl 2.94).

This splits v1's "data diversity" into two opposite effects at matched volume:

| diversity type | example | sparsity effect |
|---|---|---|
| cross-language (disjoint byte statistics) | EN/DE/ES Europarl | **raises** sparsity (94.7%) — clean neuron partitioning by regime |
| within-language register mix | wiki+books+parl | **lowers** sparsity (88.9%) — shared statistics force neurons to serve multiple contexts |

Combined with L3 (distributed within-phase knowledge) this yields a coherent account:
*neurons specialize along statistical-regime boundaries*. Languages draw sharp
boundaries (sparse, modular); registers blur them (dense, distributed). Graph
calibration on textmix-joint stays in family (subsampled Q=0.35 at β=0.5).

### 12.4 Consequences for the plan

- v2 recipe validated as language-agnostic in kind; magnitudes milder within-language.
- The sparsity law needs restating (L4 revision): capacity-to-data sets the ceiling;
  cross-regime diversity raises it, within-regime heterogeneity lowers it. Update
  pending a dedicated sweep (register-count × volume grid) if pursued.

## 13. Addendum 7 — Prefix routing: H2' resolved structurally

In the growth stack (`--grow-mult`), later-phase blocks are frozen additive modules
appended after earlier neurons, with embed/lm_head frozen and RoPE freq prefixes
preserved verbatim. Therefore **block-prefix masking is an exact router**: zeroing all
neurons after block k reproduces phase k's computation bit-for-bit.

Verified on `cl4-es_last` (×192; masks keep first N neurons/head; 30-iter eval):

| route | en | de | es | reference state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EN prefix (×128) | **2.42** | 25.08 | 20.14 | EN specialist 2.26, zero-shot 25.3/20.4 |
| DE prefix (×160) | 5.81 | **2.42** | 25.45 | cl4-de endpoint 5.40 / 2.22 / zs |
| full (×192) | 12.84 | 13.03 | 2.29 | cl4-es endpoint 12.49 / 12.60 / 2.21 |

(Δ vs references is 30-vs-100-iteration eval noise, uniform across rows.)

**Consequences:**

1. The growth stack *is* a multi-expert system out of the box:
   routed quality {en 2.26, de 2.22, es 2.22} ≈ oracle per-phase peaks, single
   checkpoint, active width ≤ ×192, zero forgetting by construction. Grok priority #1
   needs no learned gate — only a phase/domain detector at the input (byte-level
   classification being near-free).
2. Residual pollution (L2) affects only *unrouted* use of the final model — i.e., it
   prices the merge branch, not the routing branch.
3. Remaining engineering for a deployable system: label-free domain detection,
   and compression of the merged branch (Addendum 5).

### 13.1 Register replication: routed stack beats joint training outright

Textmix phases grown under the same recipe (`tmg-books` from the wiki specialist,
`tmg-parl` on top; ×128→160→224). Prefix-routing verification on `tmg-parl_last`:

| route | wiki | books | parl | reference state reproduced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WIKI (×128) | **3.05** | 12.02 | 4.39 | tm-wiki 3.09 / 12.61 / 4.29 ✓ |
| BOOKS (×160) | 6.35 | **3.24** | 4.89 | tmg-books 6.45 / 3.23 / 4.76 ✓ |
| PARL (×224) | 4.84 | 5.64 | **2.39** | tmg-parl 4.95 / 5.69 / 2.33 ✓ |

The routed system {wiki 3.09, books 3.23, parl 2.33} **beats the jointly-trained
textmix model on every domain** (joint: 3.30 / 3.28 / 2.44). Growth+routing is not a
compromise — it is the best known way to train this problem, continual or not.

### 13.2 Practical Pareto frontier (Europarl reference numbers)

| system | active width | en / de / es ppl | constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| sequential endpoint (CL-H1) | ×128 | 11.08 / 18.28 / 2.13 | none — and worst |
| merged ×3 | ×384 | 5.08 / 3.51 / 2.67 | single model, label-free |
| merged + random prune ⅓ | ≈×128 | 6.37 / 5.38 / 4.09 | single model at original capacity |
| **growth + prefix route** | ≤×192 | **≈2.26 / 2.22 / 2.22** | needs phase label at input |
| oracle specialists | ×128 each | 2.26 / 2.13 / 2.13 | upper bound |

In any continual setting (data arrives as phases), routed growth strictly dominates;
merge+prune is the label-free fallback; both crush the naive endpoint. The frontier
question left open is compression of the *unrouted* branch (Grok #2).

## 14. Addendum 8 — Compression probes: structure kills, replay revives

Two probes on the compression branch (`scripts/prune_eval.py` extended with block
granularity and checkpoint export):

### 14.1 Coherent-region removal collapses

Random pruning at identical keep=⅓, neuron-scattered vs contiguous 512-neuron blocks:

| mode | de | en | es |
|---|---|---|---|
| scattered neurons | **5.38** | **6.37** | **4.09** |
| contiguous blocks | 15.02 | 10.41 | 5.40 |

Fine-grain distribution is load-bearing: removing coherent regions destroys far more
function than removing the same count scattered. This *predicts* community-aware
pruning (whole-community removal = maximal coherent removal) would also underperform,
so that probe was skipped as falsified-by-proxy.

### 14.2 Merge → prune → tiny-recovery reaches near-joint quality at original width

Pruned-to-⅓ checkpoint (+~15 min finetune on 9 MB mixed replay from train splits,
`m3-recover`):

| system | en | de | es |
|---|---|---|---|
| prune ⅓ alone | 6.37 | 5.38 | 4.09 |
| **prune ⅓ + recovery** | **2.58** | **2.58** | **2.41** |
| (refs) full merge ×384 / joint mixed ×128 | 5.08 / 2.33 | 3.51 / 2.23 | 2.67 / 2.23 |

Recovery closes most of the prune gap at one-third the merged width, landing within
~0.3 nats of co-training. Mechanically this is **consolidation-time replay** — the
first positive result for Mechanism D in any form: a small real-token buffer applied
*after* merge+prune, not during phase training.

### 14.3 Final recipe (v2.1)

train phases (grow or rewrite) → **route by prefix** (labels available) *or*
**merge → random-scatter-prune to budget → tiny replay finetune** (single model).
Both branches now have measured, strong endpoints; remaining open items are scale
(≥4 phases) and label-free detection.

`out/logs/m3_recover.log`.

## 15. Addendum 9 — Five-phase stress test (EN→DE→ES→FR→PT)

Extends CL-H1 by two phases (`cl5-fr`, `cl5-pt`; Europarl FR/PT pairs added to the
loader). All phases 30 MB, identical schedule.

### 15.1 Interference matrix (held-out ppl)

| after phase | en | de | es | fr | pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ES (=CL-H1 endpoint) | 11.08 | 18.28 | **2.13** | — | — |
| FR | 9.42 | 20.08 | 15.47 | **2.03** | 21.63 |
| PT (final) | 8.75 | 20.36 | 8.24 | 14.32 | **2.05** |

**Surprise: forgetting is non-monotonic — later phases partially *heal* earlier ones.**
EN improves 11.08 → 9.42 → 8.75 across subsequent training; ES dips to 15.47 after FR
then recovers to 8.24 after PT. Interference in BDH is competitive-with-recovery, not
pure decay; only DE (the Germanic isolate among Romance additions) degrades
